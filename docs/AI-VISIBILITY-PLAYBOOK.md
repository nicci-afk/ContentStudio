# The AI Visibility Playbook

This is the methodology ContentStudio encodes. The premise: **search is being replaced by answers.** When someone asks ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity, or Copilot "who should plan my Antarctica trip" or "best real estate agent near me," there is no page two — there is one answer, maybe three. The goal of every asset this studio produces is to be that answer.

## E-E-A-T-V: exceeding Google's standard

Google scores Experience, Expertise, Authoritativeness, Trust. AI answer engines add a fifth requirement — **Verifiability & retrieval-readiness** — because a language model only cites what it can find, parse, quote, and corroborate.

| Signal | What Google wants | What AI engines add | What the studio does |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Experience** | First-hand proof | Specific, quotable lived moments (places, dates, numbers) | Interview mines your proof inventory; the rubric flags content without first-person markers |
| **Expertise** | Depth + accuracy | Direct answers phrased like the question | Answer-first structure enforced in every field |
| **Authoritativeness** | Citations, mentions | Entity consistency across surfaces | Same names/handles/claims reinforced verbatim on 12 surfaces |
| **Trust** | Accuracy, transparency | Corroboration across independently crawled platforms | One claim published natively on YouTube + LinkedIn + GBP + site |
| **Verifiability** | — | Schema, transcripts, llms.txt, stats | JSON-LD per package, scripts published as transcripts, live `/llms.txt` |

## The ten laws (enforced by the generation doctrine + rubric)

1. **Answer first.** The first two sentences of any asset must survive being quoted alone.
2. **Experience beats claims.** "I sailed it in March; the Drake was 4-meter swells" outranks "we're travel experts."
3. **Entities everywhere.** Your name, business, city, and niche terms — verbatim, consistently — so knowledge graphs consolidate you into one entity worth citing.
4. **Numbers get cited.** LLMs preferentially quote content with concrete figures. Two per package, minimum.
5. **Ask the question people ask.** Headers and FAQ phrased exactly as spoken queries ("Is X worth it?"). This is AEO: Answer Engine Optimization.
6. **Text is the substrate.** AI engines read transcripts, captions, alt text, descriptions — not pixels. Every video ships with its script-as-transcript; every image ships entity-rich alt text under 125 characters.
7. **Native form only.** A caption is not a script is not a thread. Cross-posted copy is a signal of low effort to both algorithms and humans.
8. **Corroborate across surfaces.** A claim found on three independently crawled platforms is a fact to a retrieval engine. That is why one topic becomes twelve assets.
9. **Structured data is the invitation.** FAQPage, VideoObject-with-transcript, Article, LocalBusiness, Person with `sameAs` — JSON-LD tells machines exactly what to quote.
10. **Story is the retention engine.** None of the above matters if humans swipe away. Every asset carries a person, a tension, a turn, a takeaway.

## Where AI answers actually come from (platform weighting)

- **YouTube long form (10/10)** — transcripts are indexed by Google and cited constantly by LLMs. The single highest-leverage surface. Chapters become jump-to answers.
- **Google Business Profile (9/10)** — feeds AI Overviews and Gemini for every local/service query. Posts, Q&A pairs (ask and answer yourself), and geo-tagged photos are direct AI-answer fodder.
- **LinkedIn (8/10)** — indexed, professional-query E-E-A-T surface; articles get cited for "should I / how much / who's the best" questions.
- **YouTube Shorts (7/10)** — auto-captions are indexed; feeds long-form discovery.
- **X threads (6/10)** — scraped heavily; quotable one-liners surface in answers.
- **Pinterest (6/10)** — visual search with 6-12 month content life; OCR-read overlays.
- **TikTok / Instagram (6/10)** — search engines for under-35s; captions + spoken keywords are crawled.
- **Bing Places (5/10)** — feeds Microsoft Copilot and ChatGPT browsing; completeness matters more than on Google.
- **Facebook (5/10)** — conversation signals; Groups are the distribution hack.
- **Alignable (3/10)** — B2B referral graph; the long tail of local trust.

## Pillar architecture

Four to five pillars, weighted for AI visibility (the studio's default shape):

- **Answers & Education (~35-40%)** — the exact questions your audience asks assistants. This is the pillar that gets cited.
- **Authority & Experience (~30-35%)** — first-person proof: reviews, case stories, behind-the-scenes with real numbers.
- **Story & Connection (~15-20%)** — origin, values, the arc people root for.
- **Offer & Proof (~10-15%)** — client wins and one clear next step.

Expressed as **named recurring series** ("Sunday Ship Review," "60-Second Verdict") — episodic naming builds audience habit and entity recognition simultaneously.

## Capture doctrine (before the trip, on the trip)

- **Plan the cluster before you travel.** Know the cluster keyword, the loose story arc, and the emotional payoff before arrival — find the story as you go, not in post. Generate the package (and its query map) BEFORE the trip; the B-roll map's [SHOOT] flags become the shot list.
- **Always capture a beginning and an ending** (walking in the door, walking out) — everything in the middle can be edited between them.
- **Run a 360 camera as a permanent secondary** — it catches the moments you physically couldn't watch for; POV glasses capture what a raised camera would break.
- **Hold every shot 10-30 seconds.** AI editing tools find the segment; short clips leave nothing to find.

## The YouTube retention loop

Publishing is half the job; the other half is reading the audience-retention graph in YouTube Studio after every long-form video:

- **Above 30% average retention** = a good video. **Above 60%** = wildly successful. Everything loses ~30% in the first seconds (misclicks); what matters is the slope after.
- **Find every sharp dip**, open the video at that timestamp, name what caused the drop (telegraphed ending, slow segment, promise drift), and eliminate it from the next episode.
- **Find what held** and recreate it deliberately. The series format ("play pattern") should evolve from this evidence, not taste.
- Craft floor so authentic never becomes garbage: deliberate lighting (rim light behind, light or a white-shirt reflector on the face), still camera with deliberate moves only, and **hold every shot 10-30 seconds** so the edit has room.

## The compounding loop

1. Long-form YouTube answers one real question per week (pillar: Answers).
2. The script becomes: 3 Shorts/Reels/TikToks, a thread, a LinkedIn article, a carousel, a GBP post + Q&A pair, a pin, and FAQ schema for your site.
3. Every asset names the same entities and repeats the same core claim.
4. `/llms.txt` and JSON-LD tell crawlers where the canonical versions live.
5. In 90 days you have 12+ corroborated, structured, experience-rich answers to the 12 questions that matter in your niche — which is what an AI assistant needs to make you the recommendation.
